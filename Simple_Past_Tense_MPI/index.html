THEORY: SIMPLE PAST TENSE

Master formulas, grammar rules, time markers, and verb forms

Module 1 • Grammar Theory

A. Function of Simple Past Tense

The Simple Past Tense describes actions, events, or situations completed at a definite time in the past.

1. Completed Actions Actions finished at a specific past point.

"We arrived in Bali yesterday morning."

2. Past Events Historical milestones or finished events.

"Indonesia proclaimed its independence in 1945."

3. Past Habits Routines done in the past that no longer happen.

"When I was young, I played chess every evening."

B. Affirmative Sentences (Positive Form)

Subject + Verb 2 (Past Simple) + Object / Complement
Active Structure

In affirmative sentences, always use Verb 2 (Past Form) regardless of singular or plural subject.

Example 1

"I visited my grandmother yesterday."

Subject (I) + Verb 2 (visited) + Object + Time
Example 2

"She watched a movie last night."

Subject (She) + Verb 2 (watched) + Object + Time
Example 3

"They went to Bandung last weekend."

Subject (They) + Verb 2 (went) + Complement

C. Negative Sentences

Subject + did not (didn't) + Verb 1 (Base Form) + Object / Complement
Negative Structure
⚠️ CRITICAL RULE: Because auxiliary "did" already signals past tense, the main verb MUST revert to Verb 1 (Base form)!
Example 1

"I did not visit my grandmother yesterday."

did not + visit (Verb 1)
Example 2

"She did not watch a movie."

did not + watch (Verb 1)
Example 3

"They did not go to Bandung."

did not + go (Verb 1)

D. Interrogative Sentences (Questions)

Did + Subject + Verb 1 (Base Form) + Object / Complement?
Question Structure

Start questions with Did, followed by subject and Verb 1 base form.

Example 1

"Did you visit your grandmother?"

Answers: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
Example 2

"Did she watch a movie?"

Answers: Yes, she did. / No, she didn't.
Example 3

"Did they go to Bandung?"

Answers: Yes, they did. / No, they didn't.

E. Nominal Sentences: WAS / WERE

Subject + was / were + Adjective / Noun / Adverb
Non-Verbal
I / He / She / It ➔ WAS

"I was tired yesterday."

"She was at the library."

You / We / They ➔ WERE

"They were very happy."

"We were classmates."

Negative & Question

"He was not angry."

"Were you at home?"
